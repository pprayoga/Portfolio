Background

About

I started out as a field service engineer for Solar Turbines, servicing gas turbines in the oil and gas industry across the U.S. and Indonesia. Most of what the job took, I learned in the field — a little training, a lot of improvising, and often being the only engineer in front of the customer with no one to fall back on. School never prepared me for that; the job site did. Over time I went from figuring things out alone to leading cross-functional teams through inspection, installation, and commissioning.

What stayed with me from those years is a habit: watch how people actually do the work, understand the process from the inside, and find where it breaks. That's what pulled me toward AI. Now I build agents and tools that do the same thing in software — I map how a task really gets done, then build something that reproduces it and runs it on its own. I'm now finishing an M.S. in Artificial Intelligence and looking for roles where I can turn messy, real-world processes into reliable AI systems.

InventionID — Automated Invention Extraction & Prior-Art Analysis

M.S. Capstone · Oregon State University · team of 3

Capstone LLM agents RAG AWS Bedrock

An agentic pipeline that reads scientific papers and finds potentially patentable ideas — mimicking how a patent analyst works. It runs a multi-stage flow (PDF parsing → summarization → embeddings → patent-query generation → prior-art retrieval), uses RAG over FAISS for cited evidence, and queries Google Patents and Scholar via external APIs for automated novelty analysis. Prompt caching on long-context inference keeps token cost and latency down.

Diary Agent

LLM pipeline MCP AWS SQLite / FTS5

A personal knowledge system I built and run in production. It turns my daily journal into meeting recaps and a deduplicated to-do list through a multi-stage LLM pipeline, and exposes my diary and a tagged research-paper library to an AI assistant via a Model Context Protocol (MCP) server. Deployed on AWS behind Cloudflare, with a nightly cron recap.
